Walk into a Moroccan home and you'll probably find both: a bottle of rose water in the bathroom and a bottle of orange blossom water in the kitchen - or the other way round. Both are distilled from flowers, both are used on the skin, and both are popular with natural beauty brands. But they're not interchangeable.

## The Basics

| | Rose water | Orange blossom water |
|---|---|---|
| Flower | Damask rose (Rosa damascena) | Bitter orange blossom (Citrus aurantium) |
| Typical INCI | Rosa Damascena Flower Water | Citrus Aurantium Amara Flower Water |
| Harvest | Spring | Spring |
| Scent | Soft, floral, slightly sweet | Fresh, bright, slightly citrusy and honeyed |
| Main cosmetic use | Toner, face mist, mixing clay masks | Face and body mist, hair perfume, gentle tonic |

Both are **hydrosols**: the fragrant water collected when flowers are steam-distilled. That's very different from "rose-scented" or "orange-scented" water made by adding fragrance to water.

## Rose Water: The Classic Toner

Rose water is the floral water most customers already know. It's used:

- as a toner after cleansing,
- as a refreshing face mist during the day,
- to mix ghassoul clay into a mask,
- in toners, mists and creams as a base ingredient.

Its scent is soft and familiar, which makes it easy to sell in almost any market.

## Orange Blossom Water: The Underrated One

Orange blossom water (called "zhar" in Morocco) is used in cooking and in beauty routines. In cosmetics it works well as:

- a body and hair mist,
- a light, fresh face tonic,
- a fragrant base for gift products.

It's less common on international shelves than rose water, which is exactly why some brands use it to stand out.

**Be honest about benefits:** both waters refresh and tone the skin. Scientific evidence for specific skin effects is limited, so market them for what customers can feel and smell - freshness, softness, scent - rather than medical benefits.

## Which One Should You Choose?

- **For a classic skincare line or a first product:** rose water. It's recognized everywhere.
- **For a distinctive, fresher scent or a body/hair mist:** orange blossom water.
- **For a hammam or gift range:** both - customers like having the choice.
- **For men's or unisex products:** orange blossom often works better than rose.

## Quality Checks for Both

### 1. Distilled or flavoured?
Ask how the water is made. A real hydrosol comes from distillation. "Floral water" made from fragrance and water is a different, cheaper product.

### 2. Food or cosmetic grade?
Both waters are also sold for cooking. Make sure the product you buy is intended and documented for your use.

### 3. Preservation and shelf life
Hydrosols are water-based and can develop microbial growth once opened. Ask whether the water is preserved, with what, and what the shelf life is before and after opening. This matters even more for spray bottles used daily.

### 4. Scent consistency
The smell can vary with the harvest. Ask for a sample from the batch you'll receive.

### 5. Packaging
Store away from heat and light. For bulk, ask for food-safe or cosmetic-grade containers that close tightly.

## Buying in Bulk

Floral waters are usually supplied in 5L, 25L or larger containers, and in spray bottles for private label. Plan your volumes around the spring harvest, and keep opened containers closed and cool.

## FAQ

### Can I use orange blossom water as a toner?
Yes. It's a light, fragrant floral water that can be used as a refreshing tonic after cleansing.

### Is rose water better than orange blossom water for the skin?
Neither is "better"; they have different scents and uses. Rose water is the classic toner; orange blossom water is fresher and great for mists.

### Do floral waters need a preservative?
Water-based products can develop microbial growth once opened. Ask your supplier how the product is preserved and follow its shelf life.

### Can I mix ghassoul clay with orange blossom water?
Yes. It's a pleasant alternative to rose water for clay masks.
